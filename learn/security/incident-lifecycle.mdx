# The incident lifecycle

**The question:** prepare, identify, contain, eradicate, recover, learn: what happens at each stage?

**The short answer:** you prepare logs, runbooks and on-call before anything happens. When an alert fires you confirm it and declare an incident, then stop the spread, remove the attacker's foothold and restore clean systems. Finally, a blameless review turns what you learned into better preparation.

## The same lifecycle, three names

The six stages above are the SANS model. NIST groups the same work differently, and its 2025 revision maps it onto the Cybersecurity Framework.

| SANS stage | NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 2, 2012 | NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3, 2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation | Preparation | Govern, Identify, Protect |
| Identification | Detection and analysis | Detect |
| Containment | Containment, eradication and recovery | Respond |
| Eradication | Containment, eradication and recovery | Respond |
| Recovery | Containment, eradication and recovery | Recover |
| Lessons learned | Post-incident activity | Improvement, under Identify |

Watch the word "Identify". In SANS it means spotting an incident. In the NIST framework it means knowing your assets and risks.

## What good looks like at each stage

- **Preparation:** central logs kept long enough to investigate, an on-call rotation, runbooks for likely incidents, and contacts for legal and your cloud provider.
- **Identification:** a clear severity scale, one incident commander, and a written timeline from the first minute.
- **Containment:** short-term moves such as revoking keys and isolating hosts, taken after capturing evidence where possible.
- **Eradication:** the root cause is found and every foothold removed, not just the one you noticed.
- **Recovery:** systems rebuilt from known-good sources and watched closely as they return.
- **Lessons learned:** a blameless review within days, with owned action items that feed back into preparation.

## Pitfalls

- **Wiping before looking.** Rebuilding the host first destroys the evidence that tells you how they got in.
- **Containing too early, or too loudly.** Against a skilled attacker, a half-finished containment warns them. Scope the intrusion before you move, unless damage is ongoing.
- **Rotating only the obvious secret.** Assume everything reachable from the compromised system is exposed.
- **Postmortems that blame people.** Blame makes people hide mistakes. Ask what made the mistake easy and fix that.
