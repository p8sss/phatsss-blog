# STRIDE vs PASTA

**The question:** which threat modeling method fits your team?

**The short answer:** STRIDE is a lightweight checklist that developers apply to a data flow diagram, one feature at a time. PASTA is a seven-stage process that starts from business goals and ends with risks ranked by impact. Most teams start with STRIDE and bring in PASTA for their highest-value systems.

## PASTA's seven stages

| Stage | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Define objectives | What does the business need, and what would a breach cost? |
| 2. Define technical scope | Which components, data and dependencies are in play? |
| 3. Decompose the application | How does data flow, and where are the trust boundaries? |
| 4. Analyze threats | Who attacks systems like this, and how, according to threat intelligence? |
| 5. Analyze vulnerabilities and weaknesses | Which known weaknesses exist in this design and code? |
| 6. Model attacks | Which attack paths chain those weaknesses together? |
| 7. Analyze risk and impact | Which risks matter most, and what should be fixed first? |

Stages 3 and 4 are where the two methods overlap. You can use STRIDE inside PASTA's decomposition and threat analysis.

## They are not rivals

The methods sit at different heights. STRIDE answers "what can go wrong here?" for a component. PASTA answers "which of the things that can go wrong matter most to the business?" for a system.

A common mix: every team runs STRIDE in design reviews, and the security team runs a PASTA-style analysis once a year on the systems that hold money or sensitive data.

## Questions to ask before choosing

1. Who will run it? Developers in a design review, or a dedicated security team?
2. Who reads the output? Engineers want tickets. Leadership wants risk in business terms.
3. How often does the system change? Fast-moving code needs a method light enough to repeat.
4. What is at stake? The more a breach would cost, the more a deeper analysis pays for itself.
